What changes the job

Hold the role fixed. Six things still vary around it — the industry, the country, the seniority, the kind of company, its size, and whether the work is remote. Which of them changes what employers ask for, and which only looked like it did?

01

Measure each factor the same way: take three levels of it, hold the job family fixed, and see how far apart the resulting requirement profiles sit. Country comes out on top in both job families.

02

Before believing that, one thing about these adverts has to be faced. A country is not only a country — it is also a language, and the requirement text is what was read and grouped.

03

So run it again on English adverts only. If country's lead were real it should survive. It does not — and nothing else moves.

04

One confound invites the next. Industries are not spread evenly across countries, so is an industry step partly a country step in disguise? Blocking on country makes the industry step larger, not smaller.

05

Last, the counting rule. One employer posting the same advert hundreds of times inflates every distance at once. Give each employer one vote and the numbers shrink together, without changing places.

What this article does not settle
  • Three levels per factor, chosen for having enough adverts on both sides. A factor with more levels could rank differently on all of them.
  • The language control removes language by dropping non-English adverts, so what survives is the English-writing part of each country, not a translated whole corpus.
  • Country still moves the profile after the control. It falls from first to fourth; it does not fall to nothing.
  • Seniority and industry finish level within their intervals. The honest reading is a tie, not a winner.
