What employers ask for, when the job stays the same

Hold the role fixed, change the context around it, and see which requirements actually move. Two job families, eleven industries, and an honest account of what the numbers will and will not carry.

METHOD

How the numbers were made

Six steps, each one guarding against a specific way this kind of analysis goes wrong.

128,487 adverts → 25,800 kept. Only a missing industry removes an advert — nothing is dropped for being awkward.
2Requirement text → 349,061 single requirements. So two demands written in one sentence are counted as two.
3Each requirement → 384 numbers. So wording stops mattering and meaning starts: Kenntnisse in SQL and SQL knowledge land together.
4Numbers → 594 groups. Learned once, on one period, so a later month can be compared rather than re-invented.
5Groups → core and shell. A group's share in one cell against the job family's own baseline.
6Every number, twice. Once per advert, once per distinct advert. Both are published.

        Which employers actually repeat themselves?

          What these numbers will not carry
          • These are advertised requirements, not the labour market. Posted is not the same as existing, and large employers are over-represented by construction.
          • Extraction finds about 80% of requirements, and misses them unevenly. Every figure here is a share of adverts, never a count of requirements per advert.
          • One requirement type — domain knowledge — is correctly identified 27% of the time. Any claim resting on it carries that number.
          • A third of the uncatalogued demand has no reason assigned, and part of it is our own grouping rather than a gap in the catalogue.
          • Every magnitude is a range. The consistent shape of these results is a stable direction over an unstable size.

          Every figure regenerates from the analysis scripts. Twenty-five known defects are tracked with their measured sizes; none of them moves a headline, which is the most persuasive thing this study can say.
